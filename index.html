🌱 What is LIME?

Before trying the experiment, let's understand the idea behind it.

LIME stands for Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations. It is an Explainable AI technique that helps people understand why a machine learning model made a particular prediction.

🔍 The problem LIME helps solve

Some machine learning models are difficult to interpret. They may predict whether a student qualifies for a scholarship, for example, but do not automatically explain why that result was produced.

LIME focuses on one selected prediction. It examines nearby examples and uses a simpler, interpretable model to estimate which input features influenced that prediction locally.

📍

Local

It explains one particular prediction rather than describing every decision made by the model.

💡

Interpretable

It presents the explanation in a simpler form, helping people understand the features involved.

🧩

Model-agnostic

The method can be used with different types of predictive models, provided their predictions can be queried.

In simple words: A prediction tells us what a model decided. LIME helps us investigate why it may have made that particular decision.

Behind the Idea

⚙️ How does LIME work?

At a high level, LIME builds an explanation around the specific example we want to understand.

1

Choose a prediction

Select one example whose prediction you want to investigate.

2

Create nearby examples

Perturb the input features to generate examples close to the selected case.

3

Obtain predictions

Use the original predictive model to see the predictions for the nearby examples.

4

Fit a simple local model

Give greater importance to examples closer to the selected case and fit an interpretable approximation.

5

Inspect the explanation

Review the features that appear to support or oppose the selected prediction locally.

➕

Positive influence

A feature may support the prediction being explained.

➖

Negative influence

A feature may count against the prediction being explained.

⚖️

Local, not universal

An explanation for one example may not describe another example or the model as a whole.

Why It Matters

💚 Why is LIME useful?

Understanding a prediction can help people evaluate whether a model's behaviour makes sense and investigate unexpected results.

🏥

Healthcare

Explore which input features contributed to an individual model prediction.

🎓

Education

Demonstrate how a model might explain an individual student's predicted outcome.

💳

Finance

Investigate the features behind a model's individual risk or approval prediction.

One important limitation: LIME produces an approximate, local explanation. Results may change depending on the sampled examples, settings, and model. An explanation should not automatically be treated as proof that a prediction is correct or fair.
